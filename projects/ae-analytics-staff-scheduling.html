The business question

What is the cost of a fairer operator schedule that also meets daily skill requirements? Separately, what patterns in an A&E dataset are associated with length of stay and four-hour breaches?

This Group K coursework contains two distinct case problems: university mainframe operator scheduling and A&E patient-data analysis. The scheduling model does not estimate hospital staffing needs. The results are team findings.

The approach

01 / Define constraints

Availability, commitments and coverage

Translate operator availability, minimum weekly hours and 14 hours of daily coverage into integer and binary decision variables.

02 / Compare options

Cost, fairness and technical skills

Find a minimum-cost schedule, assess workload fairness and extend the model to require at least six daily hours of programming and troubleshooting coverage.

03 / Make analysis usable

Patient analysis and a shared interface

Explore A&E arrival and length-of-stay patterns, compare breach classifiers and present analysis and scheduling through Streamlit.

What the analysis found

Skill coverage was feasible for a small modelled cost increase.

Weekly cost increased from £1,755 to £1,784: £29, or about 1.65%. This is a comparison of optimisation solutions, not a measured saving from a live deployment.

The minimax scenario minimises the largest weekly workload; its returned cost is not a separately proven minimum-cost objective.

The report's classification workflow uses a stratified 70:15:15 split, class weighting and validation threshold selection targeting recall of at least 0.75. That target should not be presented as an achieved test result.

Decision implications

  • Present the cost of fairness and skill coverage explicitly so a stakeholder can choose between feasible schedules.
  • Keep the scheduling decision separate from the patient-data findings.
  • Evaluate false positives alongside recall when discussing breach classifiers.

Scope and limitations

This is group coursework, not a validated clinical decision system. Patient analysis is retrospective. Investigation and treatment counts may not be available at arrival, so the model should not be presented as an arrival-time risk predictor without checking feature timing.

The public repository documents reduced-tuning execution checks and modelling limitations. Those checks do not reproduce every performance claim from the original report. Patient records and identifiers are not distributed with this case study.

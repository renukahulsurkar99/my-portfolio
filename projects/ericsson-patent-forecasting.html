The business question

How can historical patent activity inform medium-term workload and budgeting decisions, and does forecasting separate patent groups improve the total forecast?

This University of Manchester group coursework combined exploratory analysis, annual forecasting and segmentation. The findings below summarise the supplied report; they are team results.

The approach

01 / Understand the portfolio

Explore history and composition

Check records, aggregate annual counts, and examine patent types, technology keywords and title characteristics.

02 / Compare forecasting choices

Evaluate stability and holdout accuracy

Compare a last-value benchmark, Linear Regression, Random Forest and XGBoost using chronological holdouts and rolling-origin validation. Use lagged features for annual prediction.

03 / Test added complexity

Segment, forecast and aggregate

Group patent titles using TF-IDF, SVD and K-Means; compare this with clustering structured patent features. Forecast each group and sum its predictions.

What the analysis found

The preferred model depends on the decision criterion.

The last-value benchmark was most stable across rolling validation windows. XGBoost had the strongest fixed-holdout result. Segmentation helped describe the portfolio but did not beat the strongest aggregate forecast.

On the fixed holdout, the report gives XGBoost a test RMSE of 163.01 and Random Forest 183.72. Comparing those test values with the rolling-validation averages would mix different evaluation tasks.

The non-text clustering was highly imbalanced: 98.59% of records fell into one group. This limits the practical richness of the segmentation even when the groups appear statistically separated.

Decision implications

  • Choose a planning baseline using repeated validation performance, then review stronger holdout models as alternative scenarios.
  • Use segmentation to investigate portfolio composition and technology themes rather than assuming it improves annual totals.
  • Add uncertainty ranges and relevant external drivers before using forecasts for operational commitments.

Scope and limitations

This is a coursework study, not an implemented Ericsson forecasting system. Tens of thousands of patent records become a small annual modelling sample after aggregation and lag construction.

The public data guide records coverage only through 30 September 2025. Treat the 2025 count as a partial-year observation; a flat forecast anchored on it needs particular caution. Patent-event definitions and reuse terms also need verification.

Forecast errors can compound over multiple steps, small clusters create noisy series, and model rankings can change between validation and test windows.

The business question

How can historical patent activity inform medium-term workload and budgeting decisions, and does forecasting separate patent groups improve the total forecast?

This University of Manchester group coursework combined exploratory analysis, annual forecasting and segmentation. The methods and results below follow the published GitHub pipeline and saved outputs; they are team results.

My contribution

I contributed to the forecasting modelling and report writing. The wider analysis and findings shown here were produced as group coursework.

The approach

01 / Understand the portfolio

Explore history and composition

Check records, aggregate annual counts, and examine patent types, technology keywords and title characteristics. The 30,118 records produce 48 annual modelling rows after lag construction.

02 / Compare forecasting choices

Use chronological validation and testing

Compare Linear Regression, Random Forest and XGBoost using lagged inputs. Train through 2010 for validation in 2011–2016, then refit through 2016 for testing in 2017–2025. The pipeline selects its future-forecast model using validation RMSE.

03 / Test added complexity

Segment, forecast and aggregate

Group titles using TF-IDF, 50-component SVD and K-Means; compare with clustering of standardised structured features. Both schemes select two clusters. Forecast each cluster using Linear Regression and Random Forest, then sum predictions to annual totals.

What the analysis found

Validation selection and test performance gave different answers.

Linear Regression had the lowest validation RMSE, so the pipeline selected it for future forecasts. Random Forest had the lowest test RMSE among the three published models. The linear model's unstable and sometimes negative test predictions show why validation selection alone was insufficient.

Neither segmented Random Forest variant improved on its non-segmented test RMSE. Segmentation reduced the unstable Linear Regression model's test error, but its segmented results remained weaker than non-segmented Random Forest.

The non-text clustering was highly imbalanced: 29,742 records (98.75%) fell into the dominant group and 376 (1.25%) into the smaller group. The text split contained 27,511 and 2,607 records. These groupings provide descriptive portfolio structure, with limited support for stronger forecasting claims.

Decision implications

  • Report model selection and later test performance separately. Random Forest was the strongest test-period comparator in this run, while the validation-selected linear model was unstable.
  • Use segmentation to explore portfolio composition; these published comparisons do not show an improvement over the best non-segmented test result.
  • Before operational use, add simpler forecasting benchmarks, rolling-origin evaluation and uncertainty ranges, then repeat the comparison using transformations fitted only on historical training data.

Scope and limitations

This is group coursework, not an implemented Ericsson forecasting system. The 30,118 patent records become only 48 annual modelling rows after aggregation and lag construction.

The dataset ends on 30 September 2025. Its 2025 count is a partial-year observation and should not be interpreted as a full-year decline or a directly comparable annual forecast target. The patent-event definition and original data provenance remain to be verified.

Task 2 holdout predictions use actual earlier-year observations as lagged inputs. Future five-year forecasts recursively use prior predictions and keep the latest observed keyword shares fixed. Those are different evaluation tasks, so holdout errors do not establish multi-year forecast accuracy.

Task 3 fits TF-IDF, SVD, scaling and clustering across the complete patent history before evaluating the annual forecasts. Future-period information therefore influences the representation; segmented comparisons are retrospective rather than a strictly historical backtest.

The current published pipeline does not implement a last-value benchmark or rolling-origin model selection. These remain proposed improvements.

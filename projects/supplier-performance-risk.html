The business question

Which suppliers should a fashion accessories retailer prioritise, and what improvements should it seek from weaker performers?

This group coursework evaluated twelve suppliers across product quality, customer service, delivery and cost. It combined performance scoring with efficiency benchmarking and improvement targets to support a differentiated procurement recommendation.

My contribution

MOLP analysis and report writing

My work focused on the multiple-objective linear programming (MOLP) component and report writing. The project used weighted goal programming to translate efficiency benchmarks into supplier improvement priorities. The MCDA, DEA and overall findings are presented as group work.

The approach

01 / Define performance

Build product quality and service indices

Use MCDA to combine quantitative attributes and staff survey judgements into Product Quality and Customer Service scores.

02 / Benchmark suppliers

Compare efficiency and peer assessments

Use input-oriented variable-returns-to-scale DEA to compare price, late delivery, shipping errors and lead time against purchase volume, quality and service. Check the interpretation with CCR cross-efficiency.

03 / Turn gaps into priorities

Apply weighted goal programming

Use the DEA reference sets to define targets for suppliers C, F, J and L, then minimise equal-weighted deviations from those targets in the MOLP component.

What the analysis found

An efficiency label alone was not enough to choose suppliers.

Supplier H led on quality and service, while G was DEA-efficient largely through low input use. The procurement recommendation therefore considered quality, service and peer robustness alongside efficiency.

H ranked first for Product Quality (0.7434) and Customer Service (0.6591). H and B also had the strongest reported average cross-efficiency scores, 0.937 and 0.911.

The weighted goal-programming results identified lead time as the most persistent improvement gap across the four inefficient suppliers.

Decision implications

  • Use separate sourcing strategies for core procurement, low-cost orders and suppliers needing improvement.
  • Turn efficiency gaps into concrete improvement discussions, particularly about lead time.
  • Review the weighting of goals with management before treating the optimisation priorities as final.

Scope and limitations

This is a coursework recommendation, with no claim that the retailer implemented it or achieved measured savings.

MCDA utility scales and weights reflect modelling assumptions and management statements. The DEA model uses only twelve suppliers and seven input/output variables, which limits discrimination and can classify many suppliers as efficient.

Weighted goal programming uses equal deviation weights. Different management priorities could change the preferred improvement trade-offs. The analysis should be repeated when supplier performance or business priorities change.
